                                    00What it is, in plain words
                                    
  
                                    A Kardling card is a 384 × 576 PNG. Its top sixteen rows are its band: the card’s whole record, byte for byte, laid into the red, green and blue of the pixels where anyone can see them — never hidden in the picture. Everything below is its face, a pixel-art creature — its Kardling — drawn from the card’s own facts. A reader redraws the face from the band and refuses the card if a single pixel differs, so what you see is what it carries.
                                    
  
                                    What it carries is a mind: a recipe for a scorecard of at most six readable rules over one department’s records — telecom customers who leave, shop sessions that end in a purchase, bank calls that open a deposit, adults with an income over 50K a year, credit clients who default. The recipe is sealed by its maker. On your device it grows into a working mind on the real records, offline, and must clear its bar on GATE — cases it never grew on — or it is cut and abstains, always. Nothing in it is neural; you can read every rule.
                                    
  
                                    It also carries its life: who issued it, every owner it was handed to, every level it earned and every new skin, each event signed by whoever had the right to sign it and chained to the one before. A level is earned, never printed: a new search must beat its mind by 0.002 on GATE, and the receipt carries exactly what it takes for anyone to re-run it.
                                    
  
                                    A character you kit out, and take into the arena and the raids
                                    
  
                                    Every card comes with an inventory: two node slots (a third opens when it levels, a fourth at rare) and two tool slots. A node is another card you hold from the same business, for a department your Kardling does not cover; equipped, it answers with your Kardling, and it joins only if it measurably adds. A tool is one of the estate’s apps whose own tests passed on GitHub’s machines at the commit it names; it rides with your Kardling and never changes a score. A better kit genuinely scores better, and the page shows what each slot adds, measured.
                                    
  
                                    Then you play. In the arena, two cards meet on 64 cases both owners’ signatures choose, each fighting as it is kitted; the one that ranks the cases better wins, and anyone re-runs the duel from its receipt. In the raids, the bosses are the problems no search has solved: a department with no card (strike it; the first strike to clear the bar seals that department’s first card, the loot), or a whole business at a bar no lone card reached (a world boss: only a party of cards, by their owners’ consent, can fell it, and the party’s record names each member’s measured share). Nothing is staked, nothing is paid for, and every win, level and share is a receipt that re-runs.

                                    What makes a card good to hold
                                    
  
                                    Not a promise about money — this page never names a sum. What a card has is what it can show, and anyone can check it:
                                    
  
                                      
    
                                    • It works. Its mind answers real cases of its department, with its GATE score re-grown on your device to the millionth.
                                      • 
    
                                    • Its rarity is earned. A card’s stage is the number of levels in its life, and every level counts only once it re-runs. Stage 0 is common, 1–2 uncommon, 3–5 rare, 6–9 epic, 10 and up legendary — the face shows it, and the face cannot lie.
                                      • 
    
                                    • Its lineage is real. A fused card exists only if it beat both its parents on cases they were judged on. Fusions of two departments of one business did best: in organ 4, every one of the 40 fused cards kept beat both parents on cases nobody had grown or judged on.
                                      • 
    
                                    • It is credited. In a mesh, a card’s measured share of the work is credited to its owner, in a ledger anyone can replay. Credits are an accounting, not money.
                                      • 
    
                                    • It is scarce by record. There is one genesis drop; one card per serial per maker; and a card cannot be given twice — the first recorded give wins.

                                    The words this page uses
                                    
  
                                    
    
                                    Band
                                     
                                    — the card’s top 16 pixel rows: its dossier, byte for byte, in the open.
                                    
    
                                    Face
                                     
                                    — the picture below, drawn from the card’s facts by the PixelWhisperer art engine.
                                    
    
                                    Kardling
                                     
                                    — the creature on a card’s face. The card’s own facts draw it, so the same card always shows the same Kardling, and a reader that redraws a different one refuses the card. The game is Kardlings, named for who live on its cards.
                                    
    
                                    Dossier
                                     
                                    — the card’s whole record: its sealed genome and its signed, chained life.
                                    
    
                                    Genome
                                     
                                    — the mind’s recipe, a few hundred bytes, sealed by its maker’s Ed25519 key.
                                    
    
                                    FIT · GATE · TEST
                                     
                                    — the rows a mind grows on, the rows it must prove itself on, and rows nothing in this repository ever fits or selects on.
                                    
    
                                    AUC
                                     
                                    — how often a mind ranks a real yes above a real no: 0.5 is a coin, 1 is perfect.
                                    
    
                                    Bootstrap bound
                                     
                                    — the cases are resampled thousands of times; the bound is the score a lead keeps in all but the worst 2.5% of resamples. Above zero means the lead is not luck.
                                    
    
                                    Out of fold
                                     
                                    — the GATE rows are cut into four; each quarter is scored by a coupling learned on the other three, so no row judges the coupling that learned from it.
                                    
    
                                    Level
                                     
                                    — a better mind found by search and adopted only if it beats the old one by 0.002 on GATE.
                                    
    
                                    Receipt
                                     
                                    — what a level carries so anyone can re-run it: both minds, the data’s hash, both scores.
                                    
    
                                    Registry
                                     
                                    — the ordered record of cards’ lives that settles a double sale: the first recorded give wins.
                                    
    
                                    Mesh
                                     
                                    — owned cards of one business answering as one; each card’s share of the credit is measured.
                                    
    
                                    Watch it back · read-back
                                     
                                    — a duel replayed from its own receipt, case by case, and one case opened to show the very rules of each mind that fired on it: how a head-to-head eval was won, not just that it was.
                                    
    
                                    Guild
                                     
                                    — friends standing together: each signs a join with their own key, carrying their estate, and the guild draws every estate side by side. Nobody joins anybody else; reputation only.
                                    
    
                                    Talker
                                     
                                    — a card asked in plain words: it answers yes or no from the rules that fired, says how it decides and how good it is, and otherwise says it cannot answer and what it would need. It never makes an answer up.
                                    
    
                                    Learn to ask
                                     
                                    — ten missions in asking a card that can tell you no, each marked by asking the card again the questions you asked it.
                                    
    
                                    Tournament
                                     
                                    — an evaluation league: your cards in a bracket, each match a duel that re-runs. A title for the champion, and nothing else.
                                    
    
                                    Campaign · chapter
                                     
                                    — the raids, read as chapters: each business, its uncracked departments first, then the business itself. A chapter is cleared only when every boss in it has fallen, by receipts anyone can re-run.
                                    
    
                                    Kit it for me
                                     
                                    — the Kit choosing your Kardling’s nodes by measurement: the card that lifts it most first, then the next, until none lifts it or the slots are full. It is tool selection by ablation, in the open.
                                    
    
                                    Fuse · fused card
                                     
                                    — two of your cards of one business made one: kept only if the fused card beats both parents on GATE and re-runs. It is model merging and selection; organ 4 measured it, its failed prediction kept.
                                    
    
                                    Kit · node · tool
                                     
                                    — a card’s inventory. A node is your own card of another department, coupled with it; a tool is an app item that rides with it and moves no score.
                                    
    
                                    Loadout
                                     
                                    — the kit its owner signed for the card and each node as they stand; give one away or level it, and the loadout lapses until its owner equips again.
                                    
    
                                    Duel
                                     
                                    — two cards on 64 GATE cases chosen by both owners’ signatures; the better ranking wins; its receipt re-runs.
                                    
    
                                    Raid · strike · loot
                                     
                                    — a department no search has solved, struck by signed searches from each player’s own key; the first strike to clear 0.6 seals its first card.
                                    
    
                                    World boss · party
                                     
                                    — a whole business, at its best lone genesis search plus 0.010; a party of owners’ cards strikes it together, and each member’s leave-one-out share is recorded.
                                    
    
                                    Decoder #0001
                                     
                                    — the first card: it names the exact code that reads cards and the record of the whole drop, so you can re-run the drop from it.
                                    
    
                                    Key
                                     
                                    — your Ed25519 identity, made in this tab on your first visit and kept in your key file, never in the browser’s storage. Your cards are signed to it.

                                      04The mesh: owned cards answer as one
                                      
  
                                      Cards of one business — one department each — join a mesh only with a consent their owner signed for the card as it stands (give it away, and it leaves until its new owner consents). Each must answer here and lead a coin on GATE with a bootstrap bound above zero. The mesh fields its best-proven card for each department; the rest wait on the bench with their numbers. Their scores are coupled into one answer by non-negative stacking learned on GATE. Only scores cross between cards — never a record.
                                      
  
                                      Each card’s share is measured: the mesh’s out-of-fold GATE AUC with it, minus without it, as a part of a million. When the mesh does a job, every owner is credited cases × share in a hash-chained ledger that replays from the shares. Credits are an accounting of contribution, not money — the game has no way to spend or exchange one.

                                          11White paper
                                          
  
                                          
    
                                          Abstract. A Kardling card is a 384 × 576 PNG whose top sixteen pixel rows carry, byte for byte, a canonical dossier: a living-seed genome sealed by its maker (a few hundred bytes of recipe for a scorecard mind) and a chain of events — issue, give, receipt, skin — each signed by the key entitled to it and linked by SHA-256 to the one before. The face below the band is drawn from the dossier’s facts by an integer-only art engine, so any reader can redraw it and refuse a card whose picture differs by a pixel. The mind is grown on the holder’s device from real records, judged on cases it never grew on, and changes only by levels that anyone holding the same bytes can re-run. Ownership is settled by an ordered, replayable registry; minds fuse only into cards that beat both parents; and owned cards couple into a mesh whose ledger credits each owner by measured contribution. Every claim the drop makes in public passes a claim gate that re-runs it.
                                          
    
                                          1 · The problem
                                          
    
                                          A typical digital collectible owns a pointer: a token naming a picture on a server. A typical AI product rents answers: records go out to a model someone else runs. Both separate the thing of value from the person who holds it. A Kardling card puts the value — a working mind, its ownership and its proofs — inside the object the holder keeps.
                                          
    
                                          2 · The card
                                          
    
                                          The band begins with the magic DG1 and a three-byte length, then the dossier’s UTF-8 bytes, then black to the end of the band (18,426 bytes of room). The face is paintArt(facts, theme, 384, 560): id, title, dataset, department, stage, rarity, generation and parents decide the creature, the frame and the marks. Writing is stored-deflate and deterministic; reading accepts any valid PNG of the right shape, so an editor’s re-compression still opens, and checks every chunk’s CRC before the pixels are read.
                                          
    
                                          3 · The life
                                          
    
                                          The genome is opened under its maker’s seal first (verify before parse). The issue must be signed by that maker; every later event by the owner of that moment — whoever the last give names. Each event names the hash of the one before, so nothing can be dropped, reordered or re-wrapped; each is signed over its one canonical byte form, so nothing can be altered.
                                          
    
                                          4 · The mind
                                          
    
                                          The genome carries recipes, not weights: for each department it knows, which columns, which quantile cuts, which direction and which weights make a scorecard of at most six rules (seed-library’s ribosome). Expressed on a ground, the cuts are read off its FIT rows; the mind must clear its genome’s bar (0.6 AUC) on GATE or it is cut and abstains. TEST rows are never fitted or selected on by anything in the build; only the sealed organs read them. A genesis card carries the mind its search found in fixed form, so expressing it takes milliseconds and gives the same bytes on every device.
                                          
    
                                          5 · Levels
                                          
    
                                          A level is a new search (seeded from the card’s life, so every card searches its own way) whose mind beats the current one by at least 0.002 on GATE. The receipt carries the found mind, the search, the ground’s hash and both scores in millionths. Re-running it re-expresses both minds and demands the same numbers; a deep re-run repeats the search and demands the same mind. A registry records a level only once it has re-run it, so a self-signed fake level reads as a card but never counts.
                                          
    
                                          6 · Fusion
                                          
    
                                          Within a department, the parents’ rule shapes are crossed rule by rule and, half the time, mutated once; across two departments of one business, the fused card carries both minds and a coupling learned on GATE. The owner, who must hold both, tries up to twelve fusions and seals the first fused card that beats both parents on GATE, naming both — or nothing. Cards on different grounds share no cases to judge a fused card on, so that is refused.
                                          
    
                                          7 · Ownership
                                          
    
                                          A chain of signatures proves what a card is but not what it is not: an owner can sign one life to two receivers. The registry is an append-only log of lives, each entry linked to the one before; the first recorded give wins, a forked life is refused at the event where it parts, one serial per maker, and replay() re-records every entry from nothing and demands the same entry back.
                                          
    
                                          8 · The mesh and its credits
                                          
    
                                          Cards of one business join a mesh by their owner’s consent for their current life, if they answer and lead a coin on GATE with a paired-bootstrap bound above zero. The best-proven card per department is fielded; scores — never records — are coupled by non-negative stacking learned on GATE. Each member’s share is its out-of-fold leave-one-out contribution as a part of a million; jobs credit owners cases × share in a hash-chained ledger that replays from the shares. Credits are accounting, not money.
                                          
    
                                          9 · The drop
                                          
    
                                          Every department of six public datasets was a candidate; a department was admitted if four of its first eight genesis searches cleared the bar; serials went to admitted departments in turn; every search, live or cut, is recorded. Decoder #0001 carries the best genesis mind and a drop receipt naming the hash of the frozen decoder files and the head of the genesis log. Its re-run replays the log from nothing and hashes the decoder.
                                          
    
                                          10 · The claim gate
                                          
    
                                          Public posts may claim only verified, owned, grown, offline, level, fused (its code name is born), mesh or drop — each re-run by a checker at posting time — and every number in a post must be one a claim just re-ran. A post never says a Kardling is self-aware, conscious or alive, never promises, and never mentions money.
                                          
    
                                          11 · The kit
                                          
    
                                          A card’s slots are read off its checked dossier — two node slots, a third at stage 1, a fourth at stage 3, and two tool slots — so every card ever issued has them and no card’s bytes change. A loadout is signed by the owner for the card and every node as they stand. The kitted mind is the Kardling and its nodes, coupled by non-negative stacking on GATE and judged out of fold; a node joins only if it answers, covers a department nothing fielded covers, and leads a coin with a paired-bootstrap bound above zero. A tool is an app item: one of the estate’s mintable apps, exactly as mint-an-app’s vendored records hold it, carrying the commit and the CI run that proved it; it moves no score.
                                          
    
                                          12 · The arena and the raids
                                          
    
                                          A duel is agreed by both owners: a challenge and an acceptance, each signed for a card as it stands and the hash of its loadout. The batch is 64 GATE rows ordered by the hash of both signed records, so neither side chooses it; each kitted mind is weighed on the GATE rows outside the batch and scored on it; the higher AUC wins; the receipt re-runs. A raid boss is a department of the drop’s admission record that no genesis search took past the bar; a strike is one search from a seed the striker’s key and number fix, carried with the mind it found, re-checked in milliseconds. A world boss is a whole business at its best lone genesis search plus 0.010; a party strike is a mesh of consenting owners’ cards, and its receipt names each fielded card, its owner and its share in millionths. Nothing is staked.
                                          
    
                                          13 · What was measured
                                          
    
                                          Ten organs, each sealed before it was measured (organ 10 is not fully blind: dry-run before the seal; see Proof), are in Proof, with every rule’s verdict read live from the committed records — the ones that failed included. Not everything held. One example: the shop’s TEST cases are its holiday months, and its minds rank them less well than the months they were judged on — the shop-context department’s median TEST AUC fell under the 0.6 bar its cards cleared on GATE. That result is kept as sealed, not tuned away.
                                          
    
                                          14 · Limits
                                          
    
                                          The minds are small by design: readable, fast and provable, not the most accurate models one could fit; pooled gradient-boosted trees beat them in dex-frontier’s measurements. A level is adopted on GATE, and how it holds on TEST is measured, not assumed. The registry’s keeper decides only the order of arrivals. The drop on this page is sealed with a public demo key; the real drop is sealed by its owner’s own key.

                                          12Questions
                                          
  
                                          
    
                                          Is a Kardling card an NFT?
                                          No. There is no token, no chain and no server, and nothing here is for sale. The card itself carries the mind, its ownership history and its proofs, and it runs on your device.
                                          
    
                                          If I copy the PNG, do I own a copy?
                                          A copy is the same card with the same owner: ownership is the last signed give, not possession of the file. To own it, its owner signs it to your key — and the registry records that give.
                                          
    
                                          Is the mind “AI”?
                                          It is machine learning of the plainest kind: an evolutionary search finds a scorecard of at most six threshold rules over a department’s columns, judged on cases it never saw. Nothing is neural, and you can read every rule. It does not chat; it ranks cases and says why.
                                          
    
                                          Does it really run offline?
                                          Yes. Once the page and the card’s dataset are on your device, growing and asking run with the network trapped; organ 2 measures exactly that on every genesis card.
                                          
    
                                          Why are there no hospital cards?
                                          None of the five hospital departments cleared the 0.6 bar in admission, so none entered the drop. The drop’s rules were committed before the first search; the cut searches are in the record.
                                          
    
                                          Can I fake a high stage?
                                          You can sign anything into your own card, and it will read — a card proves what it is. But a level counts only once it has been re-run: no registry records a level whose numbers do not come back exactly, so a faked stage never makes it into anyone’s record.
                                          
    
                                          What stops a double sale?
                                          The registry: the first give recorded wins, and any other chain from the same life is refused for good, naming the event where it parts.
                                          
    
                                          What does a tool do in a duel?
                                          Nothing to the score, and the page says so: the arena and the raids score minds. A tool is an app your Kardling carries for you — it opens from the Kit screen, and its item proves the commit its own tests passed at. Organ 10 checks that a kit carrying a tool scores exactly as the same nodes without one.
                                          
    
                                          What do I get for a raid?
                                          For a department nobody has solved: the first strike to clear 0.6 lets its striker seal that department’s first card, with them as its maker. For a world boss: the party’s record, which names every member card, its owner and its measured share in millionths. Nothing is paid; the record is what anyone can re-run.
                                          
    
                                          What is a mesh credit?
                                          An accounting of measured contribution, not money: a count of the work a card did in a mesh. the game has no way to spend or exchange one.
                                          
    
                                          What does the page send to anyone?
                                          Nothing. It fetches its own files from the site it came from; your cards stay in your browser, your key in the tab and your key file, and your tables in the tab unless you ask the page to keep them. Check it yourself: the page’s Content-Security-Policy (in its source) lets it connect only to its own site, so a browser would block anything else; open your browser’s network panel while you use it and every request is one of this site’s files. Organs 1 and 2 ran every card read and every growth with the network trapped, and none reached for it.
                                          
    
                                          How do I give a card?
                                          1. The person receiving it opens this page and copies their key (Your key → Copy my key) and sends it to you.
                                          2. You open the card in your collection, paste their key into Send and press Send. The page signs the give with your key, records it here, and saves the card’s new PNG.
                                          3. You send them that PNG, by any means. They open it in their Inbox: it is checked on their device and appears in their collection.
                                          4. Your copy is now history: the registry records the first give of that life, so signing the same life to someone else is refused as a double give.
                                          
    
                                          What happens when my records change?
                                          Grow the card again on the new export: its recipe is re-expressed on your newest FIT rows, so its thresholds move with your data, and its GATE score on the new records tells you at once whether it still holds. Work overnight then looks for a level on the new records. Drift is real and measured here: the shop’s TEST cases are its holiday months, and some shop minds rank them less well than the months they were judged on (organ 2).
                                          
    
                                          What does “I don’t know” mean in practice?
                                          The card abstains in two cases, and says which: its mind did not clear its bar on this ground (it is cut, so it never answers), or the case lacks a measurement its rules read. An abstention is a case for a person to decide, with nothing guessed.
                                          
    
                                          Can someone game a readable mind?
                                          Yes, as with any published scorecard: whoever knows the rules can learn which measurements move a score. That is the trade-off of a mind you can read and audit. Use a card to rank and to explain, keep a person on the decision, and keep a card’s rules to the people who need them when gaming matters.
                                          
    
                                          Can one business run cards for several departments?
                                          Yes. Each card knows one department’s records; grow one per department from each team’s own export. Two cards of the same business can fuse into a card that carries both minds — across departments every fused card organ 4 measured beat both its parents on TEST — or join a mesh that answers as one, without any team seeing another’s records.
                                          
    
                                          What if a card file is lost or damaged?
                                          The card is its life, not the file. Your collection keeps every card’s life in this browser, and Download PNG writes a fresh, identical card from it at any time; a damaged file is refused when opened, and nothing in it is used. Save your key file (Your key → Save my key file) so a lost laptop loses nothing.
                                          
    
                                          What does working overnight cost?
                                          Electricity only. A search is about a minute of one core’s work: …. Re-running a level someone else earned takes milliseconds.
                                          
    
                                          How good are the minds, compared with other models?
                                          Each card’s GATE score is on it, and the drop’s table shows every department’s median score on TEST — cases nothing in the build fitted or selected on — beside its GATE score (organ 2: the median card lost 0.002 from GATE to TEST). The minds are small by design, six readable rules; models fitted on every record pooled in one place do better, which dex-frontier measured. A business’s cards coupled into a mesh beat its best single card on TEST in five of five businesses (organ 6).
                                          
    
                                          Is the game view a different program?
                                          No. It is this page, played: the same kernels and the same buttons, arranged as screens. Every number on a screen was just computed on your device or is read from a committed record, and the screen says which. Nothing in it is paid for, rolled or timed: rarity comes from levels, levels from receipts that re-run, and the estate is a count of the cards you hold. Credits are accounting, not money. Every form, recipe and district on a screen is live: a control that is there works, and nothing on a screen is a promise.
