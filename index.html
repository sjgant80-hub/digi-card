00What it is, in plain words

A DIGI card is a 384 × 576 PNG. Its top sixteen rows are its band: the card’s whole record, byte for byte, laid into the red, green and blue of the pixels where anyone can see them — never hidden in the picture. Everything below is its face, a pixel-art creature drawn from the card’s own facts. A reader redraws the face from the band and refuses the card if a single pixel differs, so what you see is what it carries.

What it carries is a mind: a recipe for a scorecard of at most six readable rules over one department’s records — telecom customers who leave, shop sessions that buy, bank calls that sell, adults who earn over 50K a year, credit clients who default. The recipe is sealed by its maker. On your device it grows into a working mind on the real records, offline, and must clear its bar on GATE — cases it never grew on — or it is cut and abstains, always. Nothing in it is neural; you can read every rule.

It also carries its life: who issued it, every owner it was handed to, every level it earned and every new skin, each event signed by whoever had the right to sign it and chained to the one before. A level is earned, never printed: a new search must beat its mind by 0.002 on GATE, and the receipt carries exactly what it takes for anyone to re-run it.

What makes a card worth holding

Not a promise about its price — this page never names one. What a card has is what it can show, and anyone can check it:

  • It works. Its mind answers real cases of its department, with its GATE score re-grown on your device to the millionth.
  • Its rarity is earned. A card’s stage is the number of levels in its life, and every level counts only once it re-runs. Stage 0 is common, 1–2 uncommon, 3–5 rare, 6–9 epic, 10 and up legendary — the face shows it, and the face cannot lie.
  • Its lineage is real. A child exists only if it beat both its parents on cases they were judged on. Children of two departments of one business did best: in organ 4, every one of the 40 born beat both parents on cases nobody had grown or judged on.
  • It can earn credit. In a mesh, a card’s measured share of the work is credited to its owner, in a ledger anyone can replay. Credits are an accounting, not money.
  • It is scarce by record. There is one genesis drop; one card per serial per maker; and a card cannot be sold twice — the first recorded give wins.

— how often a mind ranks a real yes above a real no: 0.5 is a coin, 1 is perfect.

— the cases are resampled thousands of times; the bound is the score a lead keeps in all but the worst 2.5% of resamples. Above zero means the lead is not luck.

01Start here

  1. Press “Card me”. A genesis card is signed to your key (made in this browser on your first visit; back it up with “Back up my wallet”). It appears in your wallet.
  2. Press “Grow it”. The page fetches that card’s real dataset from this site (its size is shown), checks it is the published bytes, and grows the card’s mind on it — on your device, offline. You see its GATE score — how well it ranks the cases it never grew on, as an AUC where 0.5 is a coin and 1 is perfect — beside the one the drop declared: they match to the millionth.
  3. Press “Ask it”. It is handed a real case from TEST — cases nothing in the build ever fitted or selected on — and answers yes or no with the rules that fired — or abstains. You also see what really happened.
  4. Press “Level up”. It runs up to three searches for a better mind, in the background (a search is real work: seconds on the telecom set, a minute or more on the larger ones). If one beats it by 0.002 on GATE, the receipt is signed into the card, re-run here before it is recorded, and its face shows the new stage.
  5. Breed. Press “Card me” until you hold two cards of one business, then breed them. A child is sealed only if it beats both parents; most pairings try many crossings, and some produce nothing — that is the gate working.
  6. Hand it on. Paste a friend’s key into “Send” and you get a new PNG that is theirs. They open it in Open a card, and everything is checked again on their device.
  7. Put cards to work together in the mesh, or grow your own from your own table. Then re-run the whole drop from decoder #0001.

04The mesh: owned cards answer as one

Cards of one business — one department each — join a mesh only with a consent their owner signed for the card as it stands (sell it, and it leaves until its new owner consents). Each must answer here and lead a coin on GATE with a bootstrap bound above zero. The mesh fields its best-proven card for each department; the rest wait on the bench with their numbers. Their scores are coupled into one answer by non-negative stacking learned on GATE. Only scores cross between cards — never a record.

Each card’s share is measured: the mesh’s out-of-fold GATE AUC with it, minus without it, as a part of a million. When the mesh does a job, every owner is credited cases × share in a hash-chained ledger that replays from the shares. Credits are an accounting of contribution, not money — what a credit is worth is the business of whoever runs the mesh.

In this demo, each business’s three best genesis cards per department are handed, for the mesh only, to their own demo owners (keys from public phrases, so anyone can reproduce every consent and every credit); your own cards of that business join too, consenting with your key. Organ 6 measures this very mesh on TEST.

05Grow a card from your own table

Choose a CSV. Pick the yes/no column to predict and which value counts as yes. Every other column that is a number on every row becomes a measurement (at most sixteen). Your table never leaves this device. Levels earned on it name its hash, so they re-run wherever the same table is held — and nowhere else.

A level

A search (seed-library’s evolution: 40 candidates, 30 generations, on FIT) finds a scorecard. If it beats the mind it would replace by 0.002 on GATE, the owner signs a receipt into the card: the found mind in fixed form, the search that found it, the data’s hash, and both scores in millionths. Re-running it re-expresses both minds on the same bytes and demands the very same numbers (milliseconds); a deep re-run repeats the search and demands the very same mind.

A birth

Two cards of the same department: their rule shapes are crossed rule by rule and, half the time, changed once. Two departments of one business: the child carries both minds and answers through a coupling learned on GATE. Either way the breeder’s device tries up to twelve crossings and seals the first child that beats both parents on GATE, naming both — or nothing at all.

The mesh

Consent for this life → answers here → leads a coin on GATE (bootstrap bound above zero) → the best-proven card per department is fielded → non-negative coupling learned on GATE → each card’s share = its leave-one-out contribution, out of fold → a job credits owners cases × share in a hash-chained ledger anyone can replay.

The registry

A card’s signatures prove every event in its life is genuine. They cannot prove its owner did not also sign the same life to someone else — two buyers, two chains that both check. So every device keeps a registry — an ordered log of lives, each entry linked to the one before — and the first give recorded wins. The other chain is refused for good, naming the event where it parts. Any copy of the log re-runs from nothing; the drop’s own log is published and re-run by CI on every change.

10For your business

DIGI cards give each department a small, proven scoring mind that it owns outright. It runs on the laptops you already have, never sends a record out of the building, and every claim it makes — its score, every level, every child — can be re-run by your own auditors from the card alone.

What it does for each role

Chief executive

Your scoring stops being rent. Each team’s mind is a file you own, not a subscription, and it improves only by gains it proves on cases it never grew on. The proof travels with it, so a mind you buy, sell or breed is worth exactly what it re-runs to.

Finance

The cost case below is computed by the kernel from published prices, each sourced and dated: by hand at the National Living Wage floor, rented from the cloud model you pick (the cheapest by default), or on the machine you already run. No new hardware, no per-seat fee, no token bill — and no record handed to a processor. Setup is opening a page or hosting static files; there is no service to maintain.

Operations

Every queue — collections, retention, campaign lists — is ranked the moment the records land, with the rules that put each case at the top shown beside it. A mind that cannot clear its bar abstains rather than guess, so your people see where it knows and where it does not.

Technology and security

No server, no API key, no egress: the page and the kernels are static files. Every card is checked before it is read — CRCs, Ed25519 seals, every signature, the face redrawn and compared. The code is mutation-gated, vendored byte for byte at pinned commits, served under a strict Content-Security-Policy.

Department heads

Grow a card from your own export (a CSV, any yes/no outcome, up to sixteen measured columns). You are its maker and owner. It levels overnight on your data, couples with other teams’ cards by consent, and is credited for what it measurably adds — without any team ever seeing another’s records.

The people who use it

Six rules you can read, not a black box: each answer lists the rules that fired. The card says yes, no, or “I don’t know this case” — and the person stays the decision-maker, with the evidence in front of them.

The cost case, computed

Score a business’s cases three ways, over three years. Every price is read from data/prices.lock.json (copied from the estate’s price registry, each entry with its source and the day it was checked); the milliseconds per case are measured live in this browser on the real records.

Security, data protection and audit

  • Records never leave the device. The mind is grown and run where the data is. There is no processor to contract with and no transfer abroad: privacy by design and by default, structurally (UK GDPR Articles 5 and 25).
  • Every event is signed. Issue by the genome’s maker, every give, level and skin by the owner of that moment — Ed25519 over one canonical byte form, chained by SHA-256.
  • Verify before parse. A card is refused at the first thing that fails, before a fact of it is used; the reader is total (no input makes it throw), proven by fuzzing in the tests.
  • Key management. Your key is an Ed25519 key pair made by the browser’s own WebCrypto on your first visit and kept in this site’s IndexedDB in this browser profile — never sent anywhere. It signs your gives, levels and skins. Whoever can open this browser profile, or holds your wallet backup file, can sign your cards; nobody else can, including this site and its maker. A drop’s maker key is a file its key-holder keeps offline and uses only to sign genesis cards and their hand-outs (tools/card-me.mjs); keep it where you keep other signing keys.
  • Supply chain, checkable. No package is installed; every outside module is vendored byte for byte at a pinned commit, and node tools/vendor-check.mjs --sources proves each file against its source repository. node witness-all.mjs re-runs the mutation gate over every kernel. Both run on GitHub’s machines on every change (see the gate’s runs).
  • The audit trail. Every card carries its own signed life. The registry log orders every life and replays from nothing; the mesh ledger chains every credit and replays from the shares. Whoever keeps a log can read and share it — a device, a shared drive, or a repository — and anyone given a copy can re-run it.
  • Every number re-runs. Scores are re-expressed on the published bytes; levels and births re-run; the drop replays from decoder #0001; the organs were sealed before they were measured, and git proves the order.
  • Protected characteristics are flagged, not hidden. Some genesis departments read columns such as sex, race, age or marital status, because the public research datasets hold them (the credit set’s customer department among them). A business must not decide about people on those grounds (Equality Act 2010). Every card’s rules are shown when it grows, and a card whose rules read a protected characteristic says so in a warning beside them. Grow your own cards only from the columns you may lawfully use.

Risks, answered straight

Is a six-rule mind good enough?
— Measured, not asserted: every genesis card beat a coin on TEST, cases nothing ever fitted or selected on, and the median card lost only 0.002 of AUC from GATE to TEST (organ 2); coupled into a mesh, a business’s cards beat its best single card on TEST in five of five businesses (organ 6). Models fitted on every record pooled in one place do better still — the price of keeping records apart and rules readable. It is a ranking aid that abstains when it does not know, not an oracle; where a department’s columns do not predict the outcome, no card is minted (the hospital departments did not clear the bar, so the drop has none).
What if a key is lost?
— Its cards can no longer be signed onward. Back the wallet up (Wallet → Back up my wallet) and keep it with your other credentials.
Can a card be sold twice?
— A card alone cannot prevent it, so the registry does: the first recorded give wins and the second is refused for good.
Do levels overfit?
— Measured in organ 3: 42 of 69 levels rose on TEST, but the average gain was not distinguishable from zero. So a level is a gain proven on GATE; weigh it on your own fresh cases before you lean on it. The prediction that it would carry failed, and it is kept on the page as failed.
Who holds the order of the registry?
— Whoever keeps the log. It cannot forge a single event into a card, and any copy re-runs from nothing; ordering is the one thing it is trusted for.

11White paper

Abstract. A DIGI card is a 384 × 576 PNG whose top sixteen pixel rows carry, byte for byte, a canonical dossier: a living-seed genome sealed by its maker (a few hundred bytes of recipe for a scorecard mind) and a chain of events — issue, give, receipt, skin — each signed by the key entitled to it and linked by SHA-256 to the one before. The face below the band is drawn from the dossier’s facts by an integer-only art engine, so any reader can redraw it and refuse a card whose picture differs by a pixel. The mind is grown on the holder’s device from real records, judged on cases it never grew on, and changes only by levels that anyone holding the same bytes can re-run. Ownership is settled by an ordered, replayable registry; minds breed only into children that beat both parents; and owned cards couple into a mesh whose ledger credits each owner by measured contribution. Every claim the drop makes in public passes a claim gate that re-runs it.

1 · The problem

A typical NFT owns a pointer: a token on a chain naming a picture on a server. A typical AI product rents answers: records go out to a model someone else runs. Both separate the thing of value from the person who holds it. A DIGI card puts the value — a working mind, its ownership and its proofs — inside the object the holder keeps.

2 · The card

The band begins with the magic DG1 and a three-byte length, then the dossier’s UTF-8 bytes, then black to the end of the band (18,426 bytes of room). The face is paintArt(facts, theme, 384, 560): id, title, dataset, department, stage, rarity, generation and parents decide the creature, the frame and the marks. Writing is stored-deflate and deterministic; reading accepts any valid PNG of the right shape, so an editor’s re-compression still opens, and checks every chunk’s CRC before the pixels are read.

3 · The life

The genome is opened under its maker’s seal first (verify before parse). The issue must be signed by that maker; every later event by the owner of that moment — whoever the last give names. Each event names the hash of the one before, so nothing can be dropped, reordered or re-wrapped; each is signed over its one canonical byte form, so nothing can be altered.

4 · The mind

The genome carries recipes, not weights: for each department it knows, which columns, which quantile cuts, which direction and which weights make a scorecard of at most six rules (seed-library’s ribosome). Expressed on a ground, the cuts are read off its FIT rows; the mind must clear its genome’s bar (0.6 AUC) on GATE or it is cut and abstains. TEST rows are never fitted or selected on by anything in the build; only the sealed organs read them. A genesis card carries the mind its search found in fixed form, so expressing it takes milliseconds and gives the same bytes on every device.

5 · Levels

A level is a new search (seeded from the card’s life, so every card searches its own way) whose mind beats the current one by at least 0.002 on GATE. The receipt carries the found mind, the search, the ground’s hash and both scores in millionths. Re-running it re-expresses both minds and demands the same numbers; a deep re-run repeats the search and demands the same mind. A registry records a level only once it has re-run it, so a self-signed fake level reads as a card but never counts.

6 · Breeding

Within a department, the parents’ rule shapes are crossed rule by rule and, half the time, mutated once; across two departments of one business, the child carries both minds and a coupling learned on GATE. The breeder, who must own both, tries up to twelve crossings and seals the first child that beats both parents on GATE, naming both — or nothing. Cards on different grounds share no cases to judge a child on, so that is refused.

7 · Ownership

A chain of signatures proves what a card is but not what it is not: an owner can sign one life to two buyers. The registry is an append-only log of lives, each entry linked to the one before; the first recorded give wins, a forked life is refused at the event where it parts, one serial per maker, and replay() re-records every entry from nothing and demands the same entry back.

8 · The mesh and its credits

Cards of one business join a mesh by their owner’s consent for their current life, if they answer and lead a coin on GATE with a paired-bootstrap bound above zero. The best-proven card per department is fielded; scores — never records — are coupled by non-negative stacking learned on GATE. Each member’s share is its out-of-fold leave-one-out contribution as a part of a million; jobs credit owners cases × share in a hash-chained ledger that replays from the shares. Credits are accounting, not money.

9 · The drop

Every department of six public datasets was a candidate; a department was admitted if four of its first eight genesis searches cleared the bar; serials went to admitted departments in turn; every search, live or cut, is recorded. Decoder #0001 carries the best genesis mind and a drop receipt naming the hash of the frozen decoder files and the head of the genesis log. Its re-run replays the log from nothing and hashes the decoder.

10 · The claim gate

Public posts may claim only verified, owned, grown, offline, level, born, mesh or drop — each re-run by a checker at posting time — and every number in a post must be one a claim just re-ran. A post never says a DIGI is self-aware, conscious or alive, never promises, and never mentions a price.

11 · What was measured

Eight organs, each sealed before it was measured, are in Proof, with every rule’s verdict read live from the committed records — the ones that failed included. Not everything held. One example: the shop’s TEST cases are its holiday months, and its minds rank them less well than the months they were judged on — the shop-context department’s median TEST AUC fell under the 0.6 bar its cards cleared on GATE. That result is kept as sealed, not tuned away.

12 · Limits

The minds are small by design: readable, fast and provable, not the most accurate models one could fit; pooled gradient-boosted trees beat them in dex-frontier’s measurements. A level is adopted on GATE, and how it holds on TEST is measured, not assumed. The registry’s keeper decides only the order of arrivals. The drop on this page is sealed with a public demo key; the real drop is sealed by its owner’s own key.

12Questions

Is a DIGI card an NFT?

It keeps what NFTs promised — unique, ownable, tradeable, with provenance — without the pointer. The card itself carries the mind, its ownership history and its proofs, and it runs on your device. There is no chain and no server.

If I copy the PNG, do I own a copy?

A copy is the same card with the same owner: ownership is the last signed give, not possession of the file. To own it, its owner signs it to your key — and the registry records that give.

Is the mind “AI”?

It is machine learning of the plainest kind: an evolutionary search finds a scorecard of at most six threshold rules over a department’s columns, judged on cases it never saw. Nothing is neural, and you can read every rule. It does not chat; it ranks cases and says why.

Does it really run offline?

Yes. Once the page and the card’s dataset are on your device, growing and asking run with the network trapped; organ 2 measures exactly that on every genesis card.

Why are there no hospital cards?

None of the five hospital departments cleared the 0.6 bar in admission, so none entered the drop. The drop’s rules were committed before the first search; the cut searches are in the record.

Can I fake a high stage?

You can sign anything into your own card, and it will read — a card proves what it is. But a level counts only once it has been re-run: no registry records a level whose numbers do not come back exactly, so a faked stage never makes it into anyone’s record.

What stops a double sale?

The registry: the first give recorded wins, and any other chain from the same life is refused for good, naming the event where it parts.

What do mesh credits pay?

Credits are an accounting of measured contribution, not money. What a credit is worth, if anything, is decided by whoever runs the mesh.

What does the page send to anyone?

Nothing. It fetches its own files from the site it came from; your key, cards and tables stay in your browser. Check it yourself: the page’s Content-Security-Policy (in its source) lets it connect only to its own site, so a browser would block anything else; open your browser’s network panel while you use it and every request is one of this site’s files. Organs 1 and 2 ran every card read and every growth with the network trapped, and none reached for it.

How do I give or sell a card?
  1. The person receiving it opens this page and copies their key (Wallet → Copy my key) and sends it to you.
  2. You open the card in your wallet, paste their key into Send and press Send. The page signs the give with your key, records it here, and saves the card’s new PNG.
  3. You send them that PNG, by any means. They open it in Open a card: it is checked on their device and appears in their wallet.
  4. Your copy is now history: the registry records the first give of that life, so signing the same life to someone else is refused as a double sale.
What happens when my records change?

Grow the card again on the new export: its recipe is re-expressed on your newest FIT rows, so its thresholds move with your data, and its GATE score on the new records tells you at once whether it still holds. Work overnight then looks for a level on the new records. Drift is real and measured here: the shop’s TEST cases are its holiday months, and some shop minds rank them less well than the months they were judged on (organ 2).

What does “I don’t know” mean in practice?

The card abstains in two cases, and says which: its mind did not clear its bar on this ground (it is cut, so it never answers), or the case lacks a measurement its rules read. An abstention is a case for a person to decide, with nothing guessed.

Can someone game a readable mind?

Yes, as with any published scorecard: whoever knows the rules can learn which measurements move a score. That is the price of a mind you can read and audit. Use a card to rank and to explain, keep a person on the decision, and keep a card’s rules to the people who need them when gaming matters.

Can one business run cards for several departments?

Yes. Each card knows one department’s records; grow one per department from each team’s own export. Two cards of the same business can breed a child that carries both minds — across departments every child organ 4 measured beat both its parents on TEST — or join a mesh that answers as one, without any team seeing another’s records.

What if a card file is lost or damaged?

The card is its life, not the file. Your wallet keeps every card’s life in this browser, and Download PNG writes a fresh, identical card from it at any time; a damaged file is refused when opened, and nothing in it is used. Back up your wallet (Wallet → Back up my wallet) so a lost laptop loses nothing.

What does working overnight cost?

How good are the minds, compared with other models?

Each card’s GATE score is on it, and the drop’s table shows every department’s median score on TEST — cases nothing in the build fitted or selected on — beside its GATE score (organ 2: the median card lost 0.002 from GATE to TEST).
